1. Scenario Overview
Title: Adult Orotracheal Intubation in Emergency Department
Clinical domain: Emergency medicine / airway management
Primary objective: Secure airway in a hypoxemic adult patient with impending respiratory failure.
Simulation modality: High-fidelity mannequin with monitor and airway trainer.
Expected learners: Emergency physician, respiratory therapist, critical care nurse.

2. Learning Objectives
- Perform pre-intubation assessment and identify difficult-airway risk factors.
- Execute pre-oxygenation and rapid sequence intubation (RSI) protocol.
- Coordinate team roles and communication under time pressure.
- Confirm tube placement using multimodal checks.
- Manage post-intubation stabilization and ventilation setup.

3. Clinical Context and Initial State
Patient profile:
- Age: 58 years
- Sex: male
- Weight: 86 kg
- Height: 174 cm
- Chief complaint: severe dyspnea and altered mental status
- Working diagnosis: acute hypoxemic respiratory failure, probable pneumonia with sepsis

Initial vital signs:
- Heart rate: 122 bpm
- Blood pressure: 92/58 mmHg
- Respiratory rate: 34/min
- SpO2: 82% on non-rebreather mask (15 L/min)
- Temperature: 38.9 C
- GCS: 11 (E3 V3 M5)

Airway assessment findings:
- Reduced mouth opening
- Limited neck extension
- Mallampati class III
- Copious oral secretions

4. Team Roles
- Team leader (physician): decision making, airway strategy, final intubation attempt.
- Airway assistant: laryngoscope setup, bougie support, backup devices.
- Medication nurse: prepare and administer RSI drugs.
- Monitoring nurse: continuous vitals and alarms.
- Respiratory therapist: bag-valve-mask support, ventilator connection.

5. Required Equipment and Resources
Airway equipment:
- Video laryngoscope
- Direct laryngoscope with blades sizes 3 and 4
- Endotracheal tubes (7.0, 7.5, 8.0)
- Bougie introducer
- Suction catheter and Yankauer
- Oropharyngeal and nasopharyngeal airways
- Supraglottic airway as rescue device

Monitoring and support:
- Cardiac monitor with ECG, NIBP, pulse oximetry
- End-tidal CO2 monitor (capnography)
- Bag-valve-mask with PEEP valve
- Mechanical ventilator
- IV infusion pump

Medications (example protocol):
- Induction: ketamine 1.5 mg/kg IV
- Paralysis: rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg IV
- Post-intubation sedation: fentanyl + midazolam (titrated)
- Vasopressor support if persistent hypotension

6. Scenario Workflow
Phase A: Preparation and briefing
A1. Team identifies need for definitive airway.
A2. Assign roles and airway plan A/B/C.
A3. Prepare difficult-airway cart and confirm drug doses.

Phase B: Pre-oxygenation and optimization
B1. Position patient with head-elevated laryngoscopy position.
B2. Apply high-flow oxygen and BVM support as needed.
B3. Suction secretions and optimize hemodynamics.

Phase C: RSI execution
C1. Timeout and medication safety check.
C2. Induction then neuromuscular blockade.
C3. Laryngoscopy and tube insertion.
C4. If first pass fails, escalate according to backup plan.

Phase D: Confirmation and stabilization
D1. Confirm placement with waveform capnography.
D2. Bilateral chest rise and auscultation.
D3. Secure tube and set ventilator parameters.
D4. Post-intubation sedation/analgesia and reassessment.

Phase E: Complication handling
E1. Manage desaturation (SpO2 < 88%).
E2. Manage hypotension after induction.
E3. Recognize esophageal intubation and reattempt safely.
E4. Escalate to rescue airway if cannot intubate/cannot oxygenate.

7. Success Criteria
- Definitive airway secured within 2 attempts.
- Tube placement confirmed by sustained ETCO2 waveform.
- SpO2 recovers to >= 94% within 3 minutes after intubation.
- Team follows declared backup plan without role confusion.
- Post-intubation sedation and ventilator setup completed.

8. Critical Errors to Detect
- No pre-intubation plan for difficult airway.
- No suction readiness despite secretions.
- Intubation without ETCO2 confirmation.
- Excessive repeated attempts without oxygenation recovery.
- Failure to address hypotension post-induction.
